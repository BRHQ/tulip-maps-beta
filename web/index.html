The route, and the roadbook it becomes
What it is

Tulip Maps is a modern roadbook creator.

Draw a route on the map or bring in one you recorded, drag every point exactly where it belongs, add the notes and signs that matter, and turn the whole thing into a beautiful roadbook — the kind rally crews, tour organisers and road-trippers actually navigate by. Made by Virtual Marshal, and used on real events.

How it works

Three steps, no manual.

Draw or record

Trace the route on the map and it follows the roads — or import a drive you recorded on your phone. Town and street names fill themselves in as you go.

Tidy every turn

Drag any point to nudge it into place. Drop waypoints, notes and the official road signs exactly where they belong. Nothing locks — move it until it's right.

Share the roadbook

Each junction becomes a clear little diagram. Export a printable roadbook, a GPX that opens anywhere, or a .vmr — ready to follow, or to drop into a Virtual Marshal event.

The phone app

Drive the route. The app writes it down.

Tulip Maps on your phone is the other half of the job: it records the road as you drive it, so the roadbook you draw at home sits on the line you actually took — not on a guess from a paper map.

What's a tulip? What's a roadbook?

What is a tulip?

The little junction picture in a roadbook: a ball for the road you arrive on, an arrow for the road you leave by. Drawn with a stem and a bloom, it looks like a flower — rally folk have called them tulips since the Tulip Rally made them famous.

Read: what is a tulip diagram? →

What is a roadbook?

The navigator's book: one row per instruction — a tulip, the distance, a few words — read top to bottom from start to finish. It's how rallies, raids and grand tours navigate without a map, and it's the thing Tulip Maps exists to create.
